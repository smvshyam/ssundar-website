WHY
01 — Why it exists

The analysis was never the hard part. The defensibility was.

Every capability leader I have worked with can describe their problem. What they cannot do, on the timeline the business actually runs on, is produce the artefact that makes the problem actionable at board level — the one where the methodology is named, the sample size is stated, and the confidence in each finding is explicit.

So the work goes outside. Six to twelve weeks later a PDF arrives. The numbers cannot be edited, the method is proprietary, and every follow-up question restarts the meter.

The dependency is the product.

Treeng inverts that. You keep the workbook. You keep the formulas. You can interrogate any number in it, change an assumption, and watch the conclusion move. That is not a convenience feature — it is the difference between a report you present and a report you can defend.

02 — The part nobody else does

Every finding tells you how much to trust it.

A consultant will not tell you which of their conclusions is thin. The engagement is priced on confidence. A system has no such incentive.

A

Solid. Sufficient sample, clean inputs, statistically meaningful. Act on it.

B

Indicative. The direction is reliable; the magnitude carries a wider band. Treat as a strong signal, not a settled figure.

C

Needs more data. The engine tells you exactly which data would move it up a grade.

This is the same standard I hold my own consulting work to, and the reason the evidence page on this site carries sector-only cases with sourced figures rather than a wall of logos. If a number cannot be walked through, it does not get presented.

05 — Who it is for

Leaders who answer to a CFO without an analyst on speed dial.

L&D, HR, operations and strategy leads who are asked to justify a budget, size a gap or explain a number on a timeline that does not accommodate a procurement cycle. If you have the data and need the artefact, this is the shortest defensible path to it.

It does not replace the consulting work on this site. A diagnostic tells you what is broken; it does not rebuild the architecture underneath it. Where the finding is structural — where judgment itself is the constraint rather than the measurement of it — that is still an engagement, not a report.
