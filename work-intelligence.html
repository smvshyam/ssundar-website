01 — The opportunity

Capability is generated daily and read quarterly.

Frontline leaders generate hours of operational signal a day across five or more systems. It is captured, rarely connected, and never read as capability. What exists instead is the bucket: met target, missed target, top, middle, bottom quartile. Those answer the ranking question. They never answer the capability question for the individual.

Learning is delivered as events and measured by satisfaction in the room. The twenty-one days after a program are the highest-leverage reinforcement window in the entire cycle, and the least instrumented part of it.

Three structural shifts make this the moment. Enterprise AI is now inside the building — most organisations have an approved assistant and internal teams already working on conversation data. Behavioural architectures already exist in mature capability functions: competency models, indicators, rubrics, calibration. And operations leaders have stopped asking for more training and started asking for predictability. What is missing is not data or AI. It is the design discipline that connects the two at the level of the person.

02 — The loop

Sense. Connect. Predict. Act.

SENSE

Capability signals from quality scores, call transcripts, system behaviour, conversion, tenure, learning records and HR events — each read where it already sits.

CONNECT

One living capability record per associate, team leader and trainer, mapped to the organisation’s own competency and indicator model.

PREDICT

Readiness, risk and next-best intervention per person per week — explainable to the indicator and the timestamp.

ACT

Proactive nudges to the individual, the leader and the manager inside the tools they already use. Every correction becomes new signal.

Human-in-the-loop by design: the system recommends, leaders decide. The outputs are a capability trajectory per cohort, a weekly readiness view for every leader, and one number finance can read — attrition and ramp cost avoided.

04 — Under the hood

Sovereign, compact, built on the stack they already own.

Capability scoring is a classification problem — recognising a defined set of behavioural indicators the way a client’s best quality analysts and trainers do. That makes it well suited to compact private models and to the enterprise AI stack most clients already run. The engineering is deliberately modest. The value is in the design.

Ontology
The client’s competency model translated into observable indicators, anchor statements and evidence rules. This is the asset; everything else is plumbing.
Calibration
Real examples scored by calibrated raters under a Gage R&R-style protocol. Reliability over correctness: the system, not the individual, defines the standard.
Model layer
Either the client’s approved enterprise assistant and agents, or a compact open-weight model (7–8B class) fine-tuned on their own labelled data and served inside their tenancy. Swappable by design.
Learning loop
Every manager correction becomes new signal. Monthly re-tuning; drift and bias checked against human calibration.

No workforce data leaves the client. No external model is trained on it. The capability graph and the ontology are the enduring asset — owned by the client, on the balance sheet, not in a subscription.
