The activation — 120 days

One process.
One floor. One number.

Demonstration earns conviction faster than description. The activation takes a single process, instruments it end to end, and puts a continuous capability trajectory in front of the people who own the outcome.

The sequence

Sense. Build. Activate. Scale.

01DAYS 1–30 · SENSE
Map the signal
  • Map every signal an individual generates in one process
  • Enable data access — in writing, day one
  • Connect the first dataset
  • Capture the baseline before anything changes
  • Deliver the 30/60/90
02DAYS 31–60 · BUILD
Lock the ontology
  • Competency model translated into observable indicators and anchors
  • First labelled set calibrated under protocol
  • First capability model or agent live on one leader’s teams
  • Weekly readiness report in frontline leaders’ hands
03DAYS 61–90 · ACTIVATE
Put it in the work
  • Proactive nudges running inside existing tools
  • Assessment layer live for the leadership cohort
  • Weekly 30-minute reviews with operations and people leadership
  • Corrections feeding back as new signal
04DAYS 91–120 · SCALE
Read the value
  • Value read-out with operations, people and finance
  • Decision gate
  • Roadmap for the next three processes
  • Every artefact already handed over, weekly
The single precondition

Data access from day one, agreed in writing. It is the one thing that determines whether 120 days is enough. Everything else — tooling, stack, model choice — is negotiable and swappable. This is not.

Guardrails

What does not change during an activation.

Service levels
Zero disruption to SLAs or existing quality processes. The floor does not learn a new tool.
Incentives
No change to how people are ranked, rewarded or measured while the activation runs.
Ownership
Every artefact handed over weekly, inside the client’s own tools. Nothing is held back as leverage.
The gate
A decision at day 90. Scale, stop, or re-scope — on evidence, not on momentum.

The activation is priced against cost avoided on one floor — attrition, ramp and re-work — which means it buys on an operations budget rather than a learning budget. That distinction is usually what determines whether it happens this quarter or next year.
