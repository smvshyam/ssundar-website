01 · The leak

“It takes four months before a new hire is any use.”

You hire 100 for a requirement of 80. The loss happens before anyone is measuring.

The shortfall surfaces in a QBR as a line about refreshers and backfill, after the client has felt it. By then the penalty is booked and the team leaders have spent a quarter firefighting. The name for it is time to proficiency, and it is forecastable.

NeverSome never reach the floor. Offer-to-floor loss is absorbed as a hiring number, not a capability number.
LateSome reach it late. The seat is paid for weeks before it earns.
GoneSome leave before they are dependable. New-hire attrition is reported with tenured attrition, so its cause stays hidden.

02 · The forecast

“Will I have enough trained people on the floor in March?”

A 90-day forward view of delivery-ready seats against need.

Per account and per cohort: who will be on the floor and proficient at 30, 60 and 90 days, the first week the bench falls below 90% of need, and an honest uncertainty band. Tenured, new-hire and in-ramp attrition modelled separately, so an existing-business gap is seen as early as a ramp gap.

Runs onDialer statistics, quality scores, attendance, assessments — cheap signals already in your stack. No new platform. No audio stored. Nobody ranked.
Analysed byTreeng, SSUNDAR’s own analysis build — sixteen modules for capability and L&D data — runs on your extract during the diagnostic. It is the proof the build is real, not a product you buy.

03 · Model your bench, by geography · illustrative

Feel your number. Wherever you are ramping.

Each geography you are ramping gets its own reservoir. The water behind the dam is that geography’s delivery-ready bench; the town below it is the client. The gauge marks the seats you need and, in red, 90% of them. Seepage is attrition. Each cohort is a tributary that opens the week it graduates to the floor and reaches full flow only when it is proficient; until then its water sits in the gated holding pond — paid for, not earning. Pick your geographies, type your numbers, scrub the quarter.

Up to five at once. Each starts from what we watch first in that market — illustrative starting points to adjust, not benchmarks.
