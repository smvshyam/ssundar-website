SYSTEMS ARCHITECTURE

Every Organisation Promotes Its People to Their Level of Incompetence. Then It Leaves Them in Charge.

NOVEMBER 20, 2026 6 MIN READ

Every organisation is promoting people right now. Every one of them is being raised to their level of incompetence. Not most. Not some. Every one. And when they arrive there, nobody stops them, nobody fixes them, and nobody removes them. The organisation simply leaves them in charge.

You take your sharpest agent, your fastest analyst, the one person on the floor who never misses, and you dump them into a Team Leader seat. No rebuild of judgment. No test of whether they can do the job. A new title, a bigger headcount, and a door shut quietly behind them. The execution skill that got them promoted is now useless, and it is the only weapon they own.

They will not be fired for being mediocre. A hierarchy does not punish mediocrity. It pensions it. And they cannot coach the twenty new hires sitting under them, because nobody taught them how, and the only thing they can do is the job those twenty were hired to learn. So they do it themselves. Brilliantly, for one person. Disastrously, for a team.

So the ramp stalls. Half the batch still cannot carry a queue in month four. The bottom third walks out before day ninety. And nobody catches it. Training blames Recruiting. Operations blames Attrition. Recruiting blames the market. Every function arrives with a slide and leaves with an alibi. You find out the way organisations always find out: when the penalty lands in the QBR and the client reads it back to you.

This is not misfortune. It is negligence with an org chart.

The last promotion is always a mistake.

Do the arithmetic your promotion calendar refuses to do. You promote people for being good at their current job. So they climb for exactly as long as they keep succeeding, and they stop at one place only: the first job they cannot do. There is no other exit. The final promotion every person in your building received was, by construction, a mistake. Run that machine for ten years and every seat above the floor is occupied by someone who was promoted until they failed.

And the evidence you used was worthless. Last year's results in a job they were about to leave. Speed, personal mastery, control: that is what made your best agent the best. The Team Leader seat demands the reverse. Results through other people. Hands off the work. Her greatest strength is the precise instrument of her failure. You placed a bet on the future and paid for it with a medal for the past.

Then you leave them in charge.

Nobody gets fired for incompetence that is polite. Incompetent and agreeable is not a dismissal offence in any company on earth. It is a career path. So she stays. Six years. Eight. Until a restructure does the job no leader had the spine to do.

Understand this clearly. The plateau is not dead weight. Dead weight would be cheaper. The plateau makes decisions.

It hires in its own image, because a capable report is a threat and a compliant one is company. It takes a fortnight over decisions that need a day, then lets the bravest person in the room make them anyway. Escalations learn to route around it. A deputy does the real work and is paid for none of it. And the best people one level down look up, watch the job they are climbing toward being butchered by someone who was once brilliant, and reach the only sane conclusion. They resign. Or worse, they stay and stop being good, because being good is visibly what gets you posted to the job where you fail.

One lazy promotion becomes a layer. One layer becomes the culture. Then the culture becomes the excuse.

Then you send them to class.

The damage surfaces in the numbers and is instantly given a respectable name. Thin bench. Middle-management bottleneck. Ramp challenges. Engagement dip. And the organisation reaches for the cure it always reaches for, because it is the one cure that requires nobody senior to admit anything. A leadership programme. Two days. A framework with four quadrants. A certificate in a frame. She comes back fluent in new vocabulary to the same seat she was never tested for, and the twenty new hires are still waiting for someone who can teach them.

A leadership programme is where organisations send the incompetence they manufactured, so that nobody ever has to explain who manufactured it.

The incompetence is yours.

Every incompetent leader in your building was competent the day before someone signed their promotion. You did not hire incompetence. You produced it, one signature at a time, from the only data guaranteed not to predict it. Then you protected it, because removing a person you promoted means admitting you were wrong, and admitting it is the one competence your hierarchy never selects for.

Your org chart is not a map of talent. It is a crime scene drawn in boxes. Every box marks the exact point where a capable person was pushed one step too far, and the organisation decided it would rather bleed slowly than reverse a decision in public.

Stop promoting on what is behind them.

The current-job scorecard is a lagging indicator dressed up as judgment. It tells you everything about the job the person is leaving and nothing about the one they are walking into. Polishing it changes nothing. The decision must change tense.

Make them do the next job before they get it. Before the title moves, put the candidate inside Team Leader decisions with real consequences. Own the escalation queue for a month. Coach the bottom of the batch. Carry the call when it costs. Judge that, and only that. The appraisal is an obituary for the old job.

Read the slope, not the score. The ceiling is not visible in how good someone was. It is visible in how fast they improve at work they have never done, measured against everyone else attempting it. A star who flatlines on unfamiliar work by week three is showing you their level of incompetence while it is still a forecast, not an eight-year fact sitting on your floor.

Put a price on the plateau. A batch run by a leader who has hit the ceiling is not a soft cost. It is ramp lag on an account someone is paying for, and margin walking out of the building every month. Price it, and promotion stops being a favour handed out by HR and becomes a capacity decision the revenue owner will never again let you get wrong.

Reverse it, fast and without ceremony. A failed promotion gets undone inside a quarter, before it hardens into a layer. Not as punishment. As correction. The disgrace was never in reversing it. The disgrace is leaving it in place.

Where this breaks.

Next-level trials cost senior time, and every leadership team will swear it has none, right up until the plateau has cost it ten times more. No trial fully reproduces real authority. Some people who would have grown into the seat will stumble in the test and be held back. And reversal demands a spine most leadership teams have not yet grown. A method that hides its failure points has never been anywhere near a real floor.
