Why Your L&D Team Measures the Wrong Things

Every Fortune 500 company calculates the exact cost of their learning architecture. Almost none calculate the exact return. Instead, they proxy the return through activity metrics: Completion Rate, Hours of Training, and Participant Satisfaction.

The Illusion of Action

Activity metrics answer the question: Did people show up? They do not answer the question: Did behavior change? When L&D functions report to the board utilizing these metrics, they accidentally design a system optimized for theater, not performance. If "hours trained" is the core KPI, the system will naturally generate longer, more mandatory programs regardless of operational impact.

The Performance Architecture Shift

We do not track completion at SSUNDAR. We track escalation reductions, decision velocity, and incident compression. A leadership program that took 4 hours instead of 40—but successfully halted high-stakes escalations by 22%—is vastly superior in design.

The solution is not launching a better survey tool at the end of a module. The solution is demanding that the business owner define the precise, measurable organizational outcome before the learning architecture is drafted.
